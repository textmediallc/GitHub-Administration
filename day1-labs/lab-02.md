## Lab 2: Building a Professional GitHub Profile
**Real-World Scenario:** A hiring manager or a new teammate will almost always look up your GitHub profile before your first day. An incomplete or generic profile makes it harder for people to know who they're working with or what you're capable of.
**Why we are doing this:** A complete profile — photo, bio, and pinned work — establishes your professional identity on the platform and is often the first impression a collaborator or recruiter forms of you.

### Step-by-Step Instructions:
1. **Sign In to GitHub:** Open a browser and go to `github.com`, then sign in with your account from Lab 1.
2. **Open Your Profile Settings:**
   * Click your profile photo in the top-right corner, then select `Settings`.
3. **Add a Profile Photo:**
   * Under `Public profile`, click `Edit` next to your current photo (or the camera icon).
   * Upload a clear, professional photo and save.
4. **Complete Your Bio:**
   * In the `Bio` field, write one or two sentences describing your role and focus area (e.g., "Backend developer focused on distributed systems").
   * Optionally fill in `Company`, `Location`, and `Pronouns`.
   * *Explanation:* A short, specific bio is more useful to visitors than a blank field or a vague tagline.
5. **Save Your Changes:** Scroll down and click `Update profile`.
6. **Return to Your Public Profile:** Click your profile photo again, then select `Your profile`.
7. **Pin Your Best Repositories:**
   * Click `Customize your pins`.
   * Select up to six repositories that best represent your skills, then click `Save pins`.
   * *Explanation:* Pins are curated, not chronological — choose repositories that demonstrate range or depth, not just whatever you touched most recently.
8. **Create a Profile README:**
   * Click the `+` icon in the top-right corner and select `New repository`.
   * Name the repository exactly the same as your GitHub username.
   * Check `Add a README file` and click `Create repository`.
   * Open `README.md`, click the pencil icon, add a short introduction, and commit directly to `main`.
9. **Verify the Result:** Navigate back to `github.com/your-username` and confirm your photo, bio, pinned repositories, and profile README all display correctly.
